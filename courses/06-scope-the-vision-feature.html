Scope the vision feature

Five sessions built the parts: what the model receives, what a convolution does to it, what a first classifier confuses, which labels pay, and where the model breaks. Tonight they become one decision. Most vision features are scoped the wrong way round: a model is chosen, a camera bought, and the labelling budget discovered afterwards. The bench says the order should be reversed, because on real images the quality of the labels moved accuracy further than any model or picking choice. The evening ends with Corvane's scope note, signed.

Part 0

Where we start

Corvane has five pages of a vision plan (constructed Corvane example): an image card, a feature choice, a baseline with its error table, a labelling budget, and a set of failure slices. What it does not have is a decision anyone has signed. A scope note is that decision on one page: what the feature must do, on which images, measured how, with which labels, at what point it is switched off, and who owns it. It is the page a vendor, a plant manager and a data scientist can all read and disagree with in writing, before money is spent rather than after.

Part 1 · covers pretrained models and transfer

Start from features someone else learned

5 min live

The bench's convolution features are eight kernels written by hand. A network trained on millions of images learns its own kernels, layer upon layer, and the first layer tends to learn the same kind of thing session 2 wrote by hand: edges at several angles and blobs of colour. That is why starting from someone else's trained network usually beats starting from nothing, and why it is not the whole answer.

LiveWhat the transfer evidence says, and what it does not4 min▶

Yosinski, Clune, Bengio and Lipson (2014) measured how features move between tasks. Their abstract reports that first-layer features resemble Gabor filters and colour blobs, are "not specific to a particular dataset or task", and that features must "eventually transition from general to specific by the last layer". Transferability falls as the two tasks drift apart, yet even features from a distant task beat random starting weights, and initialising with transferred features improved generalisation in a way that lasted after fine-tuning.

Three practical shapes follow. Use the pretrained network as a fixed feature extractor and train only a small classifier on top, the bench's pattern with better features. Fine-tune the top layers on your labels. Or fine-tune everything, which needs the most labels and the most care. None of them removes the need for labels from your own camera, and none of them fixes the shifts of session 5: a network pretrained on catalogue photos still meets Corvane's lamp for the first time on the belt.

Self-studyWhere the architectures live2 min▶

Which pretrained network to start from, how LeNet, AlexNet and ResNet differ, and what a convolution saves in parameters are taught in Deep Learning, session 4 ↗, and how a network trains in session 3 ↗ there. For a scope note you need less than that: the name of the starting network, the data it was trained on, its licence, and how far that data is from your camera's images. Write those four lines down; they are the transfer equivalent of session 1's image card.

Real world

Fashion-MNIST is itself a distance. Its images are front-look catalogue thumbnails, trimmed, centred, sharpened and negated by a published pipeline. A model that learned on them has learned clean, centred garments facing one way. Session 5 measured what that costs: flipped left-right, every Sneaker and every Ankle boot on the holdout went wrong. A pretrained network carries the same kind of history in its weights, and its data card is the only place that history is written down.

Part 2 · covers label cost against model cost

Which lever moves the number

5 min live

Every vision scope meeting argues about the model. The bench measured five levers on the same real images and the same holdout, and the model was not the biggest of them. Read the levers in points of holdout accuracy, and the budget conversation changes order.

LiveReading the levers, and the order to spend in4 min▶

Label quality first. Losing it cost 27.1 points, so the first money goes to a clean, doubly labelled holdout and an agreement check on every batch. Then volume and features, which moved about the same amount on the bench (6.7 and 6.4 points); a pretrained feature extractor is the cheap way to buy the second. Picking last: worth 4.9 points at 400 labels and nothing you can distinguish from luck at 300.

These are one bench's numbers: small model, 14 x 14 greyscale garments, one seed per strategy. The ranking is the lesson, not the decimals. Your own bench, on your own camera's images, is the only place your own decimals come from, and session 4's widget is a pattern you can copy.

Self-studyQuestions to put to a vision vendor4 min▶

Every question below comes from a measurement earlier in the sessions, which is what makes it fair to ask.

  1. Who owns the holdout, and who labelled it? If the vendor labels and scores on its own test set, the number is its number. (Session 4)
  2. What is the agreement rate on doubly labelled images, by class pair? A price per label without it cannot be compared. (Session 4)
  3. What is the accuracy per class, and on which slices? One overall number hides the confused pairs. (Sessions 3 and 5)
  4. What happens under the shifts our line will produce, and how confident is the model when it is wrong? Ask for calibration, not just accuracy. (Session 5)
  5. If labels were chosen by active learning, which model chose them, and will the deployed model be the same one? (Settles; Lowell et al.)
  6. What did the pretrained network learn on, under what licence, and how far is that from our camera? (Part 1)

Label cost per frame and the sampling bounds of a label audit, for driving data, are in Autonomous Driving, session p2 ↗.

Real world

The model upgrade that bought nothing. Corvane's first quote proposes a larger network on the same 300 noisy labels (constructed Corvane example). The bench puts the two choices side by side: the feature lever between raw pixels and convolution was 1.0 point at 300 labels, and the label lever was 27.1. The scope note's job is to make that comparison before the purchase order, not in the post-mortem.

Part 3 · covers the active learning disagreement

Does smart labelling pay? The evidence disagrees

5 min live

Active learning is the promise that choosing which images to label buys more accuracy per label than choosing at random. Careful researchers have published both answers. The scope note should not pick a side on faith; it should say which side its own measurement landed on, and at what budget.

LiveWhat each side measured, and why both can be right4 min▶

For. Sener and Savarese (2018) recast active learning for convolutional networks as core-set selection and report that it "significantly outperforms existing approaches in image classification experiments by a large margin". Beck and colleagues (2021) report active learning roughly 2x to 4x "more label-efficient compared to RS with the use of data augmentation".

Caution. Settles's own survey, the field's standard reference, warns that a set bought by an active learner "is inherently tied to the model that was used to generate it", reports a study where active learning needed more labels than passive learning, and raises "publication bias". Lowell, Lipton and Wallace (2019) found that training a successor model on actively acquired data "does not consistently outperform" training on i.i.d. sampled data. Munjal and colleagues (2022) re-ran popular methods under identical settings and found "an inconsistent gain over random sampling baseline", shrinking with strong regularisation.

Why both can be right. They used different models, datasets, budgets, augmentation and baselines. Gains reported under one setting do not carry automatically to another. Nothing here settles the question, and nothing should pretend to: the defensible move is to measure it on your own images, against a random baseline drawn several times.

Self-studyHow to run the test on your own images4 min▶

Session 4's bench is the template. Fix a pool, a holdout that no strategy can reach, and a budget. Run random sampling with at least five seeds and record the spread. Run the strategy at the same budget and at two larger ones. Count a gain only where it clears the spread. Then do the step the papers above disagree about: retrain a different model on the labels the strategy bought and check the gain survives. If it does not, the labels were bought for one model, and the scope note should say so.

Real world

The model change two years later. A team bought its labels with uncertainty sampling on a small classifier, then switched to a pretrained network (constructed Corvane example). The labels were concentrated on the old model's hard cases, which were not the new model's hard cases. This is the exact situation Settles and Lowell and colleagues describe. A scope note that records the strategy, the model that chose the labels and the random-baseline result lets the next team decide whether to keep the labels or top them up at random.

Build-along

Corvane's scope note, signed

★ 22 min · everyone builds

One page, seven fields, each one pulled from a page of the vision plan the earlier sessions built. Everything below is a constructed Corvane example: the plant, the bins and the thresholds are invented to show the shape. Your numbers will differ; the fields should not.

Write the decision in one sentence, with its fallback. A vision feature that routes items needs a place for the items it is unsure about. Name it.

★ Field 1 · the decision (constructed Corvane example)DECISION Route items the camera classifies as textile to bin T. Everything else, and anything below the confidence floor, goes to the manual station as today. NOT IN SCOPE defects, contamination, colour sorting, other lines

Copy the image card's "is not" field and add the camera. From session 1. The distance between training images and camera images is the first risk on the page.

Choose the starting point and say why. From sessions 2 and 3, and Part 1 tonight.

★ Field 3 · the model (constructed Corvane example)START pretrained network as a fixed feature extractor (name, training data, licence written here) TOP a small classifier on Corvane's labels, like the bench BASELINE majority class and the session 3 baseline, both printed on every report

Paste the labelling plan. From session 4, with the lever order from Part 2: quality before volume, volume before picking.

★ Field 4 · the labels (constructed Corvane example)HOLDOUT 1,000 images, labelled first, by two people, never picked QUALITY agreement checked on 10% of every batch, by class pair ROUNDS 50 random, then batches of 50; picking strategy kept only if it clears the spread of five random seeds RECORD strategy, the model that chose the labels, every image id

Set the go-live and switch-off lines. From session 5. Numbers here are placeholders to be set from Corvane's own holdout; write the rule, then the value.

★ Field 5 · go-live and switch-off (constructed Corvane example)GO-LIVE every class above its floor on the holdout, not just the average; calibration error reported; the shift tests (dimmer lamp, flipped, rotated) run and their drop recorded SWITCH-OFF a weekly audit sample below the floor, or average confidence rising while audited accuracy falls: route all to manual

Attach the vendor's answers and sign. The six questions from Part 2, answered in writing. Then one name, one date, and the date it is read again.

★ Fields 6 and 7 · vendor and signature (constructed Corvane example)VENDOR answers to the six questions attached, dated OWNER one named person, who agreed to it SIGNED date REVIEW in 90 days, or at the first model change, whichever is first
The images the model will see are described, including what the training images are not. Session 1. The image card's last field, and the distance a pretrained network brings with it.
The features or starting network are named, with the data they learned on and their licence. Sessions 2 and 6. Hand-made kernels, or a pretrained extractor, written down with its history.
A baseline and a per-class error table are printed beside every accuracy. Session 3. The confused pairs are the ones that cost money; an average hides them.
The holdout is labelled first, by two people, and no labelling strategy can pick from it. Session 4. On the bench, label quality moved accuracy 27.1 points, more than any other lever.
Any gain claimed for a labelling strategy clears the spread of several random draws at the same budget. Session 4 and tonight. At 300 labels, 1.8 points of gain sat inside 2.0 points of luck.
Go-live is a floor per class plus a calibration check plus the shift tests, not one average. Session 5. A quarter turn took the bench model to 5.1% correct at 83.7% confidence.
There is a switch-off line, an owner who agreed to it, and a review date in their calendar. Tonight. Every other row describes the feature; this one keeps it safe after launch.

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and session 6 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · On the bench, which lever moved holdout accuracy the most?

The lever order on the bench is quality, then volume and features, then picking. That is why the holdout and the agreement check are the first lines of the labelling plan.

2 · A vendor cites a paper showing active learning beats random sampling by a large margin. What is the defensible response?

The literature has not resolved this, and the bench sits on both sides depending on the budget. The answer for your feature is a measurement, not a citation.

3 · Why does the scope note carry a switch-off line?

Confidence cannot be trusted to warn you, so an audited sample has to. The switch-off line turns that audit into a decision someone has already agreed to.
