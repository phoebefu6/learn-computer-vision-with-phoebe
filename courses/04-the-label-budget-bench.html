The label budget bench

Images are cheap. Labels are not: every one is a person looking at a picture and writing down what it is. So the real design question for a first vision feature is rarely "which model?" and usually "which images do we pay someone to label, and how many?" Tonight a bench answers it with real numbers. Same 2,000 unlabelled images, same 1,000 holdout images that nobody ever picks, same budget of 300 labels, seven ways to spend it. Every model is trained and scored in your browser while you watch, including the one built on cheap labels, which is the point of the evening.

Part 1 · covers what the bench holds still

One pool, one holdout, one budget

5 min live

The pool is 2,000 real Fashion-MNIST training images with their labels hidden. The holdout is 1,000 of the official test images, labelled, locked away, and never picked by any strategy. Every rung buys exactly 300 labels from the pool, trains the same model for the same 150 steps, and is scored on the same holdout. Only two things change between rungs: the features, and which 300 images get labelled.

LiveThe seven rungs, and what each one changes3 min▶

Rungs 1 to 4 change the features, with the same 300 randomly chosen images: the majority class (no features at all), the 196 raw pixel values, 33 hand-made measurements (row and column profiles, amount of ink, bounding box, left-right symmetry), and 288 fixed convolution features (eight 3 x 3 edge kernels, ReLU, 2 x 2 max pool, exactly the operations of session 2).

Rungs 5 and 6 change which images get labelled, on the convolution features. Uncertainty sampling starts from the same first 50 random images, trains, and then buys the 50 the model is least sure about, five more times. Diversity sampling starts from the same 50 and keeps buying the image farthest from everything already bought.

Rung 7 changes the labels themselves. Same 300 images as rung 4, but three labels in ten are written as the look-alike class a hurried labeller would pick: shirt for T-shirt, coat for pullover, sneaker for sandal. That rule is modelled, and the widget says so; the training and scoring that follow are real.

Self-studyWhy a simple model, and what that does and does not prove3 min▶

The bench trains softmax regression: one weight per feature per class, ten classes, gradient descent from zero for 150 steps. It runs in about a second in a browser, it is deterministic, and an independent numpy script reproduces every number it prints to the last image. A deep network would be more accurate and would take minutes per rung on a laptop with no GPU.

What that buys you: a fair comparison of features and of label strategies, all else equal. What it does not buy: the absolute accuracy a production model would reach. For scale, the Fashion-MNIST paper's own table reports 84.2% for scikit-learn logistic regression and 89.7% for the best support vector machine, both trained on all 60,000 images at full size. The bench uses 300 labels on 14 x 14 images, so its ceiling with all 2,000 pool labels is 82.5%. Compare rungs with each other, never with a leaderboard.

Real world

The vendor demo that was really a seed. A labelling platform shows a chart where its "smart selection" beats random by two points at the same budget. It is one run. Nobody re-drew the random sample. On tonight's bench, five random draws of 300 labels span 75.6% to 77.6%, a two-point spread from luck alone. A gain the size of the luck is not yet a gain; it is a reason to run it again.

Build-along · the bench

Seven rungs, trained in front of you

Work down the rungs in order. For each one read the verdict line first, then accuracy, then the worst class and the biggest confusion, then the strip of images it paid to label. Say out loud what changed before moving on. Rung 7 is the break button: it is the shortcut every budget meeting proposes, and the bench reports what it costs.

Rungs 1 to 4: features, same 300 random labels. Write down the four accuracies. Majority is 9.8%: the 300 random labels hold 43 Shirts, the most of any class, so it answers Shirt every time. Raw pixels reach 74.8%. Hand-made features drop to 69.4%: 33 measurements threw away more than they summarised. Fixed convolution features reach 75.8%.

Part 2 · covers uncertainty and diversity sampling

Reading the ladder: features first, then which labels

5 min live

The bench's numbers, written down so they can be argued with after class. The first lesson is in rungs 2 to 4: good features moved accuracy as much as clever picking did. The second is in the dotted lines: the luck from one random draw is as large as the gain from the cleverest picker.

LiveUncertainty sampling: label where the model hesitates3 min▶

Settles's survey of active learning names three ways to measure "unsure": least confident (the top class probability is low), margin (the top two classes are close), and entropy (the probability is spread across many classes). The bench uses margin: after each round it scores every unlabelled pool image by the gap between its two most likely classes and buys the 50 with the smallest gap.

It works by concentrating labels where the classes overlap. On the bench that means Pullover, Coat and Shirt, the three garments the confusion table keeps mixing up. It has a cold start: with only 50 labels the model's sense of "unsure" is itself unreliable, and the curve shows uncertainty behind random at 100 and 150 labels. And the survey itself carries a warning: a set bought this way "is inherently tied to the model that was used to generate it". Change the model later and the labels you bought for the old one may not be the ones the new one needed.

Self-studyDiversity sampling, and why it bought 80 sandals4 min▶

Diversity sampling picks images that cover the feature space instead of images near a decision boundary. The bench uses k-centre greedy, the core of Sener and Savarese's core-set method: repeatedly buy the pool image farthest from everything already bought. Their paper reports that it "significantly outperforms existing approaches in image classification experiments by a large margin" for convolutional networks.

On this bench it lost 3.1 points to random. The strip shows why. In 288 edge features, sandals and bags are the odd shapes: a sandal is mostly gaps and straps, a bag a large block with a handle, and each one is far from every other image. Farthest-first keeps finding them, and spends 140 of 300 labels on two classes that are easy anyway. Settles names this failure directly: uncertainty and committee methods are prone to "querying outliers", and density-weighted methods exist to stop it. The result is real; what it shows is that "diversity" depends entirely on the distance you measure it in, and a fixed-feature space is not the learned space the core-set paper used.

Real world

Two well-run studies, two answers. Beck and colleagues (2021) report active learning roughly 2x to 4x more label-efficient than random sampling when data augmentation is used. Munjal and colleagues (2022) re-ran the popular methods under identical settings and found "an inconsistent gain over random sampling baseline", shrinking to little or nothing with strong regularisation. Both are careful work. Session 6 lays the disagreement out properly; tonight's bench is one more data point, and it sits on both sides depending on the budget you read it at.

Part 3 · covers label noise and label errors

Cheap labels: the break button

4 min live

Every budget meeting eventually proposes the same shortcut: a faster, cheaper labelling pass, maybe with more labels for the money. The bench prices the quality half of that offer. Same images, same model, same holdout; only the labels change, and the accuracy loses 27.1 points.

LiveHow common are wrong labels, really?3 min▶

The bench's three-in-ten is deliberately harsh, to make the cost visible. Real benchmarks are better than that and still not clean. Northcutt, Athalye and Mueller (2021) checked the test sets of ten widely used benchmarks and found "an average of at least 3.3% errors across the 10 datasets", with at least 6% of the ImageNet validation set mislabelled. MNIST's test set was the cleanest they checked, 15 errors in 10,000. Fashion-MNIST was not one of the ten, so no error rate for it is claimed here.

Their sharper finding is about test sets, not training sets: with corrected test labels, "lower capacity models may be practically more useful", because the bigger models had partly learned the errors. Corvane's holdout is the most valuable 1,000 labels it will ever buy. That is why the plan has it labelled first, by two people.

Self-studyWhat a labelling quote should state besides price3 min▶

A price per label without an error rate is half a quote. Ask for the agreement rate on a doubly labelled sample, the class pairs where disagreement concentrates, and what happens to an image the labeller is unsure about (a skip option is worth paying for). Fashion-MNIST's own labels came from the product's silhouette code, assigned by in-house fashion experts and reviewed by a separate team: two layers, which is exactly the structure to ask a vendor to describe.

Label cost per frame, audit sampling and its bounds are taught in depth, for driving data, in Autonomous Driving, session p2 ↗. The general rule carries over: audit a sample, report the agreement, and price the rework before choosing the cheaper vendor.

Real world

The quote that doubled the labels. A team is offered twice as many labels for the same money by a vendor that skips the review pass (constructed Corvane example). On the bench, the clean 300 reach 75.8% and the curve says clean labels keep paying up to 500. The noisy 300 sit at 48.7%. Before accepting "more labels for the money", measure the agreement rate on fifty doubly labelled images. It takes an afternoon and it is the number the decision turns on.

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and session 4 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · At 300 labels, uncertainty sampling scores 77.6% against 75.8% for random labels. Can you tell Corvane that uncertainty sampling helps?

The comparison that matters is the gain against the spread of the baseline, not against one draw of it. The bench shows both, and the curve shows the gain growing past the spread as the budget grows.

2 · Diversity sampling bought 80 Sandals, 60 Bags and 7 Trousers, and scored 72.7%, below random. What went wrong?

Settles's survey warns that query strategies are prone to "querying outliers". The core-set method measures distance in a learned feature space; the bench measures it in fixed edge features, and the strip shows what that buys.

3 · A vendor offers labels at half the price with no review pass. What should the decision turn on?

Label quality is the largest lever on the bench. A price without an error rate cannot be compared with another price, and the audit that measures it costs an afternoon.
