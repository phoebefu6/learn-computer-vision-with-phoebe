Learn Computer Vision Foundations with Phoebe

A camera hands a model a grid of numbers, and everything a vision system does is arithmetic on that grid. Six sessions make the arithmetic visible: read real images as arrays, slide real kernels across real pixels, train a small classifier and find what it confuses, then spend a fixed labelling budget on a bench that trains every model in your browser. The question running through all six: what is the model actually looking at, and which labels are worth paying for? The running case is Corvane, a constructed recycling plant adding one camera to one sorting line.

The six sessions 🎯 · from a grid of numbers to a signed scope note

The teaching images are Fashion-MNIST: 70,000 real catalogue photos of clothing from Zalando Research, 28 x 28 pixels, ten classes, MIT licence. They stand in for "textiles on the line" at Corvane, and every page says so: they are garments on a clean background, not waste photos. Each session adds one page to Corvane's vision plan.

🔢 Session 1 · foundations

An image is a grid of numbers

Pixels, channels and resolution; what 0 means; brightness, contrast and flips as array operations, and the uint8 bug that makes a brightened image darker. Build-along: Corvane's image card.

▶ Start here 🪟 Session 2 · core

Slide a small window: convolution

A kernel is nine weights, multiplied and summed at every position. Edges, blur and sharpen computed on real pixels; stride, padding and pooling; and why a learned kernel beats a hand-made one.

kernels on real pixels 🧺 Session 3 · core

A first classifier and what it confuses

Raw pixels against hand-made features against a convolution feature layer, on the same labels. The confusion table, per-class error, and why shirts, pullovers and coats are the hard part.

baseline + error table 🎛️ Session 4 · bench night

The label budget bench

2,000 unlabelled images, 1,000 locked holdout images, 300 labels. Random, uncertainty and diversity sampling trained in your browser, the luck under all of them, and the cheap-labels rung that costs 27.1 points.

★ the bench 🔦 Session 5 · applied

Where it fails: slices and shift

Failure slices, a dimmer lamp, a flipped item, a quarter turn, and a model that stays confident while it is wrong. Calibration, and IoU computed on drawn boxes for detection and segmentation.

shift and calibration 📝 Session 6 · advanced

Scope the vision feature

Pretrained models and transfer, label cost against model cost, what to ask a vendor, and the open disagreement about active learning. Capstone: Corvane's signed scope note.

What is honest here 🧾

The images are real and every number about them is computed. The plant is constructed, and where the literature disagrees, the pages say so instead of choosing a side.

ClaimStatus
